QUARTERLY MARKET BRIEF - UK RETAIL PROPERTY
===PAGE===
Prime retail vacancy in the twelve largest UK city centres edged up to 9.4% in
the third quarter, the first increase in seven quarters. The rise was
concentrated in secondary pitches, where three national chains entered
administration during the period and released 41 units between them. Prime
pitches remained tight, with headline Zone A rents up 2.1% year on year and
incentive packages broadly stable at around 14 months rent-free on a ten year
term. Investors should treat the headline vacancy figure with caution: it counts
units marketed for reletting but excludes those held vacant pending
redevelopment, which we estimate would add a further 180 basis points. The
divergence between prime and secondary has now persisted for eleven consecutive
quarters and shows no sign of narrowing.
===PAGE===
Retail park performance diverged sharply from high street and should be assessed
separately rather than blended into a single retail figure. Footfall across
open-format retail parks rose 3.8% against the same quarter last year, the sixth
consecutive quarterly increase, while high street footfall fell 1.2%. Rental
growth followed, with retail park headline rents up 5.6% against 2.1% on prime
high street. The driver is occupier mix rather than consumer preference: retail
parks have absorbed the discount grocers and click-and-collect operations that
require parking and servicing high street units cannot offer. Analysts modelling
retail as a single asset class have consistently understated retail park
returns, and we expect the gap to widen through the next two reporting periods.
