QUARTERLY MARKET BRIEF - EUROPEAN LOGISTICS
===PAGE===
Warehouse vacancy across the six largest European logistics markets fell to
3.1% in the third quarter, the lowest level recorded since the series began in
2011. The decline was concentrated in the Netherlands and the Rhine-Ruhr
corridor, where speculative completions have not kept pace with take-up for
seven consecutive quarters. Prime headline rents rose 8.4% year on year, and
incentive packages shortened from an average of 9.2 months rent-free to 5.6
months. Investors should note that the vacancy figure excludes sublet space,
which we estimate adds a further 40 basis points and has been rising since
March. Occupiers signing before the end of the year are still securing terms
that will look favourable by the second quarter, but the window is narrowing
and landlords in the tightest submarkets have begun withdrawing incentives
entirely rather than renegotiating them downward.
===PAGE===
Rail freight volumes tell a different story from the warehouse market and
should not be read as a leading indicator for it. Tonne-kilometres across the
continental network fell 2.7% against the same quarter last year, the fourth
consecutive quarterly decline, driven almost entirely by bulk commodities
rather than containerised goods. Intermodal volumes were flat. The divergence
between falling rail tonnage and tightening warehouse space reflects a shift in
what is being moved rather than how much: lower-value bulk has migrated to
short-sea shipping while higher-value containerised freight, which is what
drives warehouse demand, has held steady. Analysts who treat the rail figure as
a proxy for overall freight activity have consistently underestimated logistics
property demand for six quarters running, and we expect that error to persist
through the next reporting cycle unless the reporting convention changes.
